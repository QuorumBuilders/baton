Baton helps pharmacy and chemist vendors keep patient blood pressure (BP) records and coordinate follow-up visits. Patients do not need a Baton application account. Baton is a hackathon prototype being developed for possible future use as a health-tech product.

Current SMS and voice communications are simulated. No real SMS messages or calls are delivered by the prototype. These pages describe the current application; they do not establish full compliance with Nigerian data protection law.

Who operates Baton

Baton is the product name. The project contact and general location below have been supplied by the project operator. The legal or operating name and full physical address still need verification.

Information we collect

  • Patient details: first and last names, primary Nigerian mobile number, date of birth, preferred language, optional sex and optional alternative contact number. New registrations offer English and Nigerian Pidgin.
  • Health and follow-up records: systolic and diastolic BP, optional pulse, measurement time and recording vendor; follow-up dates, fulfilment, scheduling changes, reminder schedules and vendor attention notifications.
  • Agreement and communication records: the vendor-recorded processing agreement and time for new registrations, agreement to additional vendor sharing, separate SMS and voice choices, their timestamps and subsequent changes. Older records may lack a processing agreement or earlier consent history; a legacy snapshot is not evidence of consent that was never recorded.
  • Vendor accounts: mobile number, pharmacy or chemist name, hashed password or PIN, account status and authentication dates. Administrator accounts use usernames and permissions; Django also supports optional name and email fields maintained through administration.
  • Phone verification: phone number, verification purpose, hashed OTP, issuance and expiry, failed-attempt count, verification, completion and invalidation times. Codes are not stored as plaintext in the database.
  • Communication attempts: intended phone recipient, reminder/category, selected voice language and recording reference, processing times, adapter and delivery mode, outcome and reason. A simulated outcome is not actual delivery.
  • Browser and administration information: necessary cookies, database sessions containing login and unfinished registration/measurement data, and Django administrative action records. Development server output can also contain request paths and phone numbers or communication content. See the Cookie Policy.

Purposes and lawful processing

We use patient details to identify the correct record, avoid duplicates, record BP history, associate agreed vendors and coordinate follow-ups. A primary phone number is used for lookup and new-patient phone verification; the alternative number is stored as an optional contact detail and is not the current reminder destination. The application does not collect medication names, prescriptions or dosage instructions.

For new patient health records, this prototype obtains an affirmative agreement through the registering vendor for record-keeping, follow-up coordination and access by the initial vendor. The intended consent basis is sections 25(1)(a), 26 and 30(1)(a) of the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023. An additional vendor must separately record the patient's agreement before gaining access to existing history. Reading a notice or entering an OTP is not consent to health-data processing or routine reminders.

Vendor account details and transactional verification support the registration requested by the vendor and operation of that account, with the intended contract basis under section 25(1)(b)(i). Necessary security and access-control processing requires a documented legitimate-interest assessment under section 25(1)(b)(v). Those operational assessments, controller/processor roles and any applicable healthcare confidentiality basis must be confirmed before real patient onboarding. These notices do not substitute for that review or assert an unverified legal obligation.

Communication consent is optional and separate for SMS and voice. Routine reminders currently use voice only. OTP is transactional phone verification and does not depend on routine communication consent. See Communication Consent Information.

Vendor associations and who can access records

A patient has one global record matched by their phone number. Registration creates the initial vendor association. Before another vendor is associated, discovery reveals that a record exists for the supplied number, but does not reveal the patient's name or health history. The additional vendor must confirm the patient's agreement to sharing.

All associated vendors can see the patient's complete Baton BP and follow-up history, including reminder and communication outcomes. The original recording vendor and historical entries remain recorded. An association does not transfer ownership or replace earlier history. Unassociated vendors cannot open the patient's record through patient pages.

Authorized administrators can access records through Django administration according to staff status and permissions. Vendor status alone gives no administrative privileges. People operating the development machine or database may have access outside the application, so device and operational safeguards also matter. See the Patient Privacy and Data-Sharing Notice.

Service providers and transfers

No external SMS, voice or analytics provider is integrated in the current code. Mock adapters run locally. If you email the project contact, your enquiry is handled through that email service; do not send BP records, identity documents, passwords or OTPs in an initial email.

Storage and security

Local development stores application records and sessions in SQLite on the development machine. Django provides password hashing, authenticated sessions, CSRF protection, template escaping and permission checks. Session and CSRF cookies require HTTPS when debugging is disabled. This describes configured controls, not a completed production security assessment; database encryption, production access arrangements and backup practices have not been verified.

Development SMS output includes the full intended number and message, including OTPs; mock voice output includes the number and recording/category details. Use synthetic demonstration data and protect terminal output. Development adapters refuse production use. A secure production deployment needs reviewed logging, HTTPS, device/database protection, backups and incident response.

Retention and your rights

The prototype has no automated deletion schedule and no verified retention periods. BP and follow-up entries form a historical record, and saved BP measurements have no ordinary edit/delete operation. Records are not promised to be kept indefinitely merely because the interface preserves history. Read Data Retention and Deletion for the principles and request process.

Subject to applicable law, you may request information about processing, access and a copy, correction, erasure, restriction, or portability, object to relevant processing and withdraw consent. You can contact the project or ask an associated vendor to help. Withdrawal does not undo processing that was lawful before withdrawal. Processing-agreement or sharing withdrawal requires an assessment and any necessary access restriction; it is separate from the immediate routine-channel withdrawal controls. No patient self-service rights portal or automatic deletion process is implemented.

Baton automatically schedules follow-up dates using an administrator-configured interval and flags overdue follow-ups for vendors. It does not interpret BP, diagnose, prescribe or make clinical decisions about patients. See the Medical Disclaimer.

Enquiries, complaints and updates

Use Contact and Privacy Complaints for privacy, account and rights requests. You also have the right to complain directly to the Nigeria Data Protection Commission (NDPC); contacting Baton first is not a condition of that right.

We will update this notice when verified operator details or actual practices change. Material changes should be explained before affected processing, with fresh consent where required; displaying a new notice does not itself change an existing consent record.

Legal references and review before production

Primary references are the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 and the NDPC's 2025 General Application and Implementation Directive (GAID). Health information is sensitive personal data. Production review must address lawful processing, minimization, purpose and storage limitation, accuracy, confidentiality and enforceable rights.

Before real onboarding, complete a Data Privacy Impact Assessment (DPIA) review for health records and sharing between vendors. GAID article 28(3) expressly lists sensitive or highly personal data among circumstances requiring a DPIA and filing with the NDPC; Baton should plan for that requirement, with professional confirmation of its application and any consultation needed under section 28. Confirm NDPC controller/processor registration obligations, data protection officer and audit duties, vendor responsibilities/agreements, processor contracts, handling of children or persons unable to consent, retention, requests and breach response. These remain legal and operational work to be completed.
